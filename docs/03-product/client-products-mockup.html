Two clients, one pass

The extension and the browser share an account, a quota and a rule set — but they ride two different transports, because a Chrome extension cannot speak REALITY and a bundled core can. Everything below is drawn from that one constraint. Build order is extension first; the browser reuses about seventy percent of it.

Build 1 · Extension

Options: four switches and a diagnostics button

Default routing is proxy unless told otherwise. Our user is a foreigner in China: more of the web is broken for him than works, so a direct-by-default rule set spends its errors in the worst possible place — the first page he opens.

What the two share, and where they part

Shared parts are built once for the extension and reused. The split is the transport, and it runs deeper than it looks: two transports mean two blocking surfaces, two availability targets and two on-call rotations.

What these screens do not show

Neither client covers anything outside its own window. Cursor, Slack's desktop app and anything a terminal does are unaffected. Developers will notice on day one, so it belongs on the product page, not in a footnote.

Whether per-user bytes on the extension's HTTPS inbound can be metered at all is still unverified. Until that question is answered the quota meter in states 2 and 4 above cannot be trusted, and every screen here is drawn against a number we cannot yet produce. That check is the first three days of work, before any of this is built.
